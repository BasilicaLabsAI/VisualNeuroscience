Aboutwhat this is, and what it is drawn from

Six brain states — flow, rest, focused attention, threat, reward pursuit and meditation — each drawn as a ring of fourteen regions in seven networks, with the couplings that strengthen in that state as coloured threads and the ones that fall quiet as grey dashes. Pick a state and every region that has a parcel in the atlas lights up on the scan in its ring colour; press a region or a whole network on the ring and the planes centre on it.

Every region with a parcel in the atlas lights up on the scan in its ring colour. Press a region on the ring — the small one in the box, or the full one the bar’s Network opens — and it comes forward on the scan with the regions it couples to held at half strength, or press a network’s name in the full ring for all of it. Move the crosshair on the scan and the ring follows it back.

The couplings are composites read off the imaging literature, not measured connectomes, and each state names its sources beneath the ring. Regions keep their place and colour in every state, so switching states moves the threads and nothing else. Three of the fourteen regions have no parcel in AAL-116 and wear a dashed arc: picking one parks the crosshair at a published coordinate instead.

Rendered with NiiVue (Rorden lab). MNI152 template, McConnell Brain Imaging Centre. AAL parcellation, Tzourio-Mazoyer et al. 2002.
