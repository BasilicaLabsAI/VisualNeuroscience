The two faces are a line map of the classic Brodmann scheme, descending from Brodmann’s own 1909 figures: every area outlined and numbered, nothing drawn beneath. Pick an area and it fills, on the face that shows it. Anterior is on the left in the external view and, as the scheme is conventionally drawn, on the right in the internal one. The drawing is a schematic — where each area actually sits in a real head, with the atlas’s own boundaries, is what the On the scan view shows. Brodmann numbered the cortex in 1909 by how its cells are layered, not by what it does — which is why the numbers wander about the brain and why several of his 52 have no agreed human counterpart. The 41 drawn here are those the shipped atlas labels; 12–16, 31, 33 and 49–52 are absent. Each area is bilateral: Brodmann described cortical types, not hemispheres, so where a function is strongly one-sided the note says so rather than the atlas splitting the area. The boundaries are an approximation to begin with — they were drawn from a handful of post-mortem brains, and no two people's cortex is folded the same way. This particular volume descends from the Talairach Daemon, whose labels were grown outwards to fill the brain: 14% of what it marks lies in white matter that the AAL atlas does not count as cortex at all, and label 48 has become a catch-all for the peri-Sylvian region rather than the small area Brodmann gave that number to. Read a boundary as a neighbourhood, not a border. Educational, not diagnostic.
