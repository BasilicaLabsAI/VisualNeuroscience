What it is

A tractogram is a reconstruction of the brain’s wiring, built from diffusion MRI: the scanner measures which way water molecules are free to move in each voxel, and since water travels more easily along a nerve fibre than across it, those measurements trace out the paths that bundles of axons follow. Each thread here is a streamline, an estimate of one such path through the white matter.

The colours are not tissue types or measurements — they say which way a fibre is running at that point, the standard convention in the field:

left – right front – back up – down

Drag to turn the brain, and pinch or scroll over the view to zoom. Slice, at the left of the bar, puts a slider on each edge of the view, each a cut taking the streamlines and the brain away from that side: draw a slider back to its centre mark to undo its cut, Reset slice under View undoes them all, and Slice turned off shows everything whole. Brain shows or hides the MNI152 brain around the fibres, drawn as the 3D Region Atlas draws it, the MRI showing at a cut face as it does there; Tract shows or hides the streamlines; Thicken raises a tall bar that makes the brain more solid or more see-through; View turns the brain to one of its faces, or home to the view the page opens with; and Revolve sets it turning until you take hold of it. The page remembers how you left it: the brain and how solid it is, the streamlines, the cuts and the view. This is a population average rather than any one person’s brain, so it shows the pathways people generally share.
