What it is

A tractogram is a reconstruction of the brain’s wiring, built from diffusion MRI: the scanner measures which way water molecules are free to move in each voxel, and since water travels more easily along a nerve fibre than across it, those measurements trace out the paths that bundles of axons follow. Each thread here is a streamline, an estimate of one such path through the white matter.

The colours are not tissue types or measurements — they say which way a fibre is running at that point, the standard convention in the field:

left – right front – back up – down

Or, with By bundle under Bundles in the box beside the view, each of the 46 bundles the atlas names takes a colour of its own, as the field’s atlases draw them, and a tap on a fibre names its bundle whichever colouring is on.

Drag to turn the brain, and pinch or scroll over the view to zoom. Slice, at the left of the bar, puts a slider on each edge of the view, each a cut taking the brain, and the streamlines with it, away from that side: draw a slider back to its centre mark to undo its cut, Reset slice under View undoes them all, and Slice turned off shows everything whole. Brain shows or hides the MNI152 brain around the fibres, drawn as the 3D Region Atlas draws it, the MRI showing at a cut face as it does there, and Transparency raises a tall bar that makes it more see-through or more solid. Tract shows or hides the streamlines. Slice tract, on whenever Slice is turned on, cuts them with the brain, and off leaves them whole while the brain alone is cut; Offset stands their cut ends out of the brain’s cut face and Distance sets how far, in millimetres, both greyed while the brain is off; and Thicken sets how thick they are drawn. View turns the brain to one of its faces, or home to the view the page opens with, Revolve sets it turning until you take hold of it, and Export, at the end of the bar, offers the streamlines and the brain as a 3D model. The region box beside the view, the 3D Region Atlas’s own, folds away on its tab: highlight a region there and only the streamlines that run through it are drawn, and with Highlight the regions ticked the region itself stands among them in its colour, cut as the brain is, and shows on the brain’s cut face as it does on the 3D Region Atlas. Highlight two or more and the switch turns to Connecting, only the streamlines that run between them, or back to All through, every streamline through any of them; a press on a region’s name opens its note. Under the regions, Bundles lists the bundles the atlas names, by system: association, projection, commissural, cerebellar and the cranial nerves. L and R show or hide each side of a bundle, the switches a whole system or every bundle, and a press on a name shows that bundle alone, a second press every bundle again; a tap on a fibre names its bundle in a small window, whose Alone does the same. The fewer streamlines are drawn, the more finely they are drawn, smoothed into curves and rounder, and where they crowd they are shaded darker, which shows their depth. The page opens with the brain on at 65 per cent transparency, the streamlines on and nothing sliced, and remembers the rest of how you left it: the streamlines’ switches, offset and thickness, the regions highlighted with their two switches, the bundles shown and their colouring, and the view. This is a population average rather than any one person’s brain, so it shows the pathways people generally share.
