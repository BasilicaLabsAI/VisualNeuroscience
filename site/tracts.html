What it is

A tractogram is a reconstruction of the brain’s wiring, built from diffusion MRI: the scanner measures which way water molecules are free to move in each voxel, and since water travels more easily along a nerve fibre than across it, those measurements trace out the paths that bundles of axons follow. Each thread here is a streamline, an estimate of one such path through the white matter.

The colours are not tissue types or measurements — they say which way a fibre is running at that point, the standard convention in the field:

left – right front – back up – down

Drag to turn the brain, and pinch or scroll over the view to zoom. Slice, at the left of the bar, puts a slider on each edge of the view, each a cut taking the brain, and the streamlines with it, away from that side: draw a slider back to its centre mark to undo its cut, Reset slice under View undoes them all, and Slice turned off shows everything whole. Brain shows or hides the MNI152 brain around the fibres, drawn as the 3D Region Atlas draws it, the MRI showing at a cut face as it does there, and Transparency raises a tall bar that makes it more see-through or more solid. Tract shows or hides the streamlines. Slice tract, on whenever Slice is turned on, cuts them with the brain, and off leaves them whole while the brain alone is cut; Offset stands their cut ends out of the brain’s cut face and Distance sets how far, in millimetres, both greyed while the brain is off; and Thicken sets how thick they are drawn. View turns the brain to one of its faces, or home to the view the page opens with, and Revolve sets it turning until you take hold of it. The page remembers how you left it: the brain and its transparency, the streamlines with their switches, offset and thickness, the cuts and the view. This is a population average rather than any one person’s brain, so it shows the pathways people generally share.
