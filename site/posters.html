Microanatomy · skeletal formulas

The Neurotransmitters

Seventeen molecules, from a gas of two atoms to a chain of five amino acids, every one drawn at the same bond length so their sizes compare, with what each does and the family it belongs to; beneath them, where they come from: the catecholamines from tyrosine, the indolamines from tryptophan, GABA from glutamate, acetylcholine made and unmade, nitric oxide from arginine.

Skeletal formulas drawn with RDKit from SMILES, with formulas and average molar masses computed for the forms drawn; the words are the Microanatomy page’s.

Microanatomy · affinities

The Receptors

A hundred and eight receptors, grouped by the transmitter each answers to, with how tightly each one holds it: binding constants and potencies on a common scale that runs in tenfold steps, further right holding tighter, beside the coupling of each receptor and its natural ligand, and why channels at the synapse let go where G-protein-coupled receptors hold on.

Affinities from the IUPHAR/BPS Guide to PHARMACOLOGY (Harding SD et al., Nucleic Acids Res 2024), every number traced to its study; the database is under the Open Database Licence 1.0 and its contents under CC BY-SA 4.0.
