The atlas

An interactive atlas of the human brain: the MNI152 head under the AAL-116 parcellation, the Brodmann areas searchable by what they do, whole-brain tractography, receptor densities, network states and published studies drawn on the scan — free, offline, and rendered on your own device. I believe it is the most advanced and most usable brain atlas on the App Store: the deep atlases live in research infrastructure like the EU’s EBRAINS, built for laboratories, and nothing there or on the store puts this much of the brain in your hands, readable without training.

The developer

I am Fadil Karim. I finished a PGCert in Applied Neuroscience at King’s College London and could not carry it on to the full master’s — full-time work and the cost got in the way. So I kept learning by building the atlas I had wanted to study from. One person, at OttomanLabs, with a short feedback loop: write to fid@ottomanlabs.ai and it usually ships within the week.

How it was built

By directing Anthropic’s Claude models as the implementation engine — Opus 4.8, then Opus 5, then Fable 5 and Fable 5.1 — across roughly twenty thousand lines of shipped code. I owned what to build, which data to trust and whether it met the bar; the AI wrote most of the code; nothing shipped unverified, and no scientific claim without its published source. The “.AI” in the name is how it was made before it is anything it does.
