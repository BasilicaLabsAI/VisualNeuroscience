# Superior Frontal Gyrus — Functions, Receptors and Evidence

Oct 17, 2026 · @Fadil Karim

## At a glance

The superior frontal gyrus (SFG) is the top strip of each frontal lobe. AAL's Frontal\_Sup region covers its outer (dorsolateral) face; the medial face and the supplementary motor area (SMA) are separate AAL regions ([Tzourio-Mazoyer et al., 2002](https://doi.org/10.1006/nimg.2001.0978)). It is not one functional unit: the back is dorsal premotor cortex; damage to the lateral part just in front of it (mostly area 8) impaired working memory under heavy load in one lesion study; and parts of the front join the default mode network ([open it in the Region Atlas](https://visualneuroscience.ai/regions.html#sel=P%3AFrontal_Sup)).

| Choosing and preparing movements | Back of the gyrus (dorsal premotor cortex, area 6) | [Hoshi & Tanji, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1038/35044075); [Ruland et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08528-4) |

Laughter evoked by electrical stimulation, the SMA syndrome after surgery and much of the response-inhibition literature come from the SMA and medial wall next door. They are covered below and flagged as neighbouring territory.

## Anatomy and boundaries

Frontal\_Sup runs from the precentral sulcus at the back to the front of the frontal lobe, between the superior frontal sulcus and the top edge of the hemisphere. AAL drew it by hand on one MNI brain with sulci as landmarks, so it is a landmark label, not a functional or cytoarchitectonic unit; its authors note it does not remove the need for probabilistic anatomical or cytoarchitectonic maps ([Tzourio-Mazoyer et al., 2002](https://doi.org/10.1006/nimg.2001.0978)).

- **Architecture, back to front.** The gyrus passes from premotor cortex (area 6) through area 8 and area 9 to area 10 at the frontal pole ([Visual Neuroscience, BA6](https://visualneuroscience.ai/regions.html#sel=B:6); [BA8](https://visualneuroscience.ai/regions.html#sel=B:8); [BA9](https://visualneuroscience.ai/regions.html#sel=B:9); [BA10](https://visualneuroscience.ai/regions.html#sel=B:10)). Area 9, on the upper mid-dorsolateral surface, lacks a well-developed layer IV; area 8B lies just behind it on the dorsomedial surface ([Petrides & Pandya, 1999](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1460-9568.1999.00518.x)).
- **Premotor areas, 2025 maps.** Mapping ten post-mortem brains, the Jülich group found seven premotor areas. The three dorsal ones (6d1–6d3) are agranular; 6d2 covers the caudal SFG from near the midline down to the upper bank of the superior frontal sulcus, where it meets 6d3, which lies only in the sulcus's depth; 6d1 lies further back, toward the precentral gyrus. The superior frontal sulcus is the landmark that separates dorsal from ventral premotor cortex ([Ruland et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08528-4)).
- **Coordinates.** Mean centres of 6d2 are −16, 1, 66 (left) and 16, 4, 67 (right); 6d3 sits at −23, 2, 53 and 25, −1, 54, in Colin27 space ([Ruland et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08528-4)).
- **Subregions by connectivity.** Diffusion tractography in 20 adults split the SFG into anteromedial, dorsolateral and posterior subregions, each with its own network. That study's SFG included the medial surface and the SMA, so only its dorsolateral subregion roughly matches Frontal\_Sup ([Li W et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.04.011)). The Brainnetome atlas uses seven SFG subregions per hemisphere: A8m, A8dl, A9l, A6dl, A6m, A9m and A10m ([Fan et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhw157)). Its A6dl appears to correspond mainly to premotor areas 6d2 and 6d3 ([Ruland et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08528-4)).
- **How AAL slices it.** Judging by their names and positions, the dorsolateral Brainnetome subregions (A6dl, A8dl, A9l) fall mostly in Frontal\_Sup and the medial ones in Frontal\_Sup\_Medial or the SMA. That is an inference from the labels, not a published overlap.
- **Neighbours.** The medial face is AAL's Frontal\_Sup\_Medial, and the back of the medial face is Supp\_Motor\_Area, which also covers the medial part of the crown at the back of the gyrus, out to about 11–19 mm from the midline on the AAL volume. Dorsal premotor cortex there is therefore split between Frontal\_Sup and Supp\_Motor\_Area ([Tzourio-Mazoyer et al., 2002](https://doi.org/10.1006/nimg.2001.0978)). The frontal eye field sits at the back edge of the gyrus, deep in the precentral sulcus just below its junction with the superior frontal sulcus ([Amiez et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4739-05.2006)).

## Movement planning

The back of the gyrus is dorsal premotor cortex, which takes part in planning movements and selecting actions ([Hoshi & Tanji, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conb.2007.02.003)). Cell-level evidence comes from monkeys; human evidence comes from lesions, TMS and imaging of premotor cortex as a whole.

- **Target plus limb (monkeys).** In macaques, neurons in the lateral dorsal premotor cortex first gather information about both the target and the arm to use; their later activity specifies the planned reach ([Hoshi & Tanji, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1038/35044075)).
- **Cue-to-movement rules (human lesions).** Patients with premotor lesions were impaired when they had to recall which of six arm movements went with each of six sensory cues, but not when the same cues pointed to six locations ([Halsband & Freund, 1990](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/113.1.207)).
- **Early selection (human TMS).** TMS over premotor cortex delayed choice responses when given 100–140 ms after the cue; over primary motor cortex it did so only at 300–340 ms. The authors conclude that premotor cortex selects movements early and that the left hemisphere dominates rapid selection ([Schluter et al., 1998](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/121.5.785)).
- **Where it sits in a living brain.** In eight volunteers analysed one by one, activity for choosing hand movements by arbitrary rules lay in the dorsal branch of the superior precentral sulcus, near the back end of the superior frontal sulcus, always above and behind the frontal eye field ([Amiez et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4739-05.2006)).
- **Its network.** In one tractography parcellation, the posterior SFG subregion, which there includes the SMA proper, linked to the precentral gyrus, caudate, thalamus and frontal operculum; at rest it tracked sensorimotor regions ([Li W et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.04.011)).

## Eye movements and attention

The frontal eye field (FEF), the main cortical area for voluntary eye movements, sits at the back edge of the SFG rather than clearly on it. In individual brains it lies deep in the ventral branch of the superior precentral sulcus, just below where that sulcus meets the superior frontal sulcus ([Amiez et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4739-05.2006)).

- **Not in area 8.** Blood-flow studies place the human FEF near the precentral sulcus or deep in the back end of the superior frontal sulcus, which challenges the textbook view that it lies in Brodmann area 8 ([Paus, 1996](<https://doi.org/10.1016/0028-3932(95)00134-4>)).
- **Premotor, per the 2025 maps.** The Jülich maps pair the FEF with premotor area 6v1 and the inferior FEF with 6v2, “not in the prefrontal cortex”. Earlier FEF peaks fell mostly in 6d1, 6v1 and 6d3, the last in the depth of the superior frontal sulcus, and the authors found no clear assignment to one area ([Ruland et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08528-4)).
- **Monkey versus human.** A review notes it is still unclear whether differences between monkey and human FEF locations reflect real anatomy, different mapping methods or individual variation ([Vernet et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnint.2014.00066)).
- **Top-down attention.** A frontoparietal system “which includes parts of the intraparietal cortex and superior frontal cortex” prepares and applies goal-directed selection of stimuli and responses ([Corbetta & Shulman, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn755)). In this literature the frontal node is usually the FEF region, not the whole gyrus.
- **A dorsal tract for space and action.** Saccades, voluntary attention, mental imagery and motor sequences cluster onto a dorsal frontoparietal network carried by SLF I, which links the superior parietal lobule to the posterior SFG. The link comes from matching tract maps to fMRI meta-analyses in different people, and SLF I's status as a separate tract is disputed ([Parlatini et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.08.031)).
- **What this means for AAL.** Group-level FEF activations can land in Frontal\_Sup, Precentral or Frontal\_Mid. Saccade findings are best reported as FEF findings, not as functions of the SFG itself.

## Working memory

Working memory under heavy load has the strongest causal evidence of any cognitive role for this region. In a lesion study, eight patients whose left prefrontal damage involved the SFG did worse on working-memory tasks than every control group, and the gap grew with task difficulty, most of all for spatial material ([du Boisgueheneuc et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awl244)).

- **Where the damage mattered.** Voxel-based lesion mapping pointed to the lateral, posterior SFG, “mostly Brodmann area 8, rostral to the frontal eye field”. The lesions were removals of low-grade gliomas, reaching into the middle frontal gyrus in four patients. The comparison groups were 11 healthy people, 5 patients with prefrontal damage sparing the SFG and 4 with right parietal damage. The authors propose that this region's part in working memory is “triggered by the highest level of executive processing” ([du Boisgueheneuc et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awl244)).
- **Spatial maintenance (fMRI).** In 11 volunteers, an area in the superior frontal sulcus held more sustained activity for spatial than for face working memory, lay in front of the frontal eye field and sat higher and further back than its monkey counterpart ([Courtney et al., 1998](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.279.5355.1347)).
- **Holding versus choosing.** In event-related fMRI, holding spatial items in mind engaged area 8 and intraparietal cortex, whereas selecting an item from memory to guide a response engaged area 46 ([Rowe et al., 2000](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.288.5471.1656)).
- **Across many studies.** A meta-analysis of 24 n-back studies (668 sets of activation coordinates) found robust activation in lateral premotor, dorsolateral and ventrolateral prefrontal, frontopolar and posterior parietal cortex; it does not single out the SFG ([Owen et al., 2005](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.20131)).
- **Intracranial recordings.** In 24 young people with drug-resistant epilepsy (aged 11–23), stereo-EEG showed high-gamma power during the memory delay widely suppressed below rest, including in the left SFG and the medial SFG on both sides. During the response, the left SFG was the main negative hub of the connectivity network ([Ervin et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/epi.70241)).
- **Specialised or general?** The fMRI result suggests a spatial specialisation, but the lesion deficit was not confined to spatial material; only its growth with load was biased towards space. The best current reading is high-load working memory with a spatial bias; that is an interpretation, not a settled finding.

## Executive and cognitive control

The right SFG is linked to proactive control: getting ready to hold back a response before any stop signal appears. In 114 adults doing a stop-signal task, those whose right SFG responded more to the expected chance of a stop signal stopped faster. The cluster, in anterior lateral area 9, also tracked a measure of motor urgency ([Hu et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1175-16.2016)). The design is correlational.

- **Reactive stopping is mainly elsewhere.** Classic lesion work tied slow stopping to damage in the right inferior frontal gyrus ([Aron et al., 2003](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1003)). Later lesion–EEG work in 16 such patients found that the damage impairs triggering the stop, not carrying it out ([Choo et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.79667)).
- **Medial damage slows stopping.** Nine patients with right superior medial frontal damage, most likely SMA and pre-SMA, had longer stop-signal reaction times than 19 controls on average, though four of the nine stopped normally ([Floden & Stuss, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn.2006.18.11.1843)). That territory lies mostly in AAL's Supp\_Motor\_Area and Frontal\_Sup\_Medial.
- **An execution network.** The dorsolateral SFG subregion connects with the middle and inferior frontal gyri, which the authors call a cognitive execution network ([Li W et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.04.011)).
- **No causal test yet.** We found no lesion or stimulation study testing whether the dorsolateral SFG itself is needed for proactive control.

## Self, introspection and the default mode network

Parts of the SFG switch on for self-reflection and switch off during demanding outward-focused tasks. In fMRI, introspective, self-related judgements activated the SFG, and the same region was inhibited during fast sensory categorisation of similar pictures and sounds ([Goldberg et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2006.03.015)).

- **How strong.** The samples were small: nine people in the visual experiment and eight in the auditory one. The authors' claim that self-related processing is suppressed during intense perception is an interpretation, not a direct measure of self-awareness ([Goldberg et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2006.03.015)).
- **Where.** The activation spanned the SFG and extended medially toward the anterior cingulate and paracingulate cortex, so it overlaps AAL's Frontal\_Sup\_Medial as well as Frontal\_Sup.
- **At rest.** Both the anteromedial and the dorsolateral SFG subregions correlate with the default mode network at rest, while the posterior subregion correlates with sensorimotor regions ([Li W et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.04.011)).
- **Social cognition.** We found no study tying theory of mind specifically to the dorsolateral SFG.

## Speech, language and the frontal aslant tract

The SFG's role in speech runs mainly through the frontal aslant tract (FAT), a white-matter pathway from the SMA complex and the lateral SFG to the inferior frontal gyrus and anterior insula ([La Corte et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2021.641586)).

- **Where it starts.** Tractography and dissection place FAT origins on the outer SFG just in front of the precentral gyrus, with none found on the medial surface ([Briggs et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/ca.23523)). In 105 Human Connectome Project subjects, about 98% of FAT streamlines arose from the crown of the SFG rather than its medial wall, although the authors still conclude that the tract arises from both surfaces ([Tagliaferri et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00429-024-02778-4)). Earlier work described the tract as linking Broca's region with the pre-SMA and anterior cingulate ([Catani et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awt163)).
- **Fluency.** In 35 people with primary progressive aphasia and 29 controls, FAT damage tracked verbal fluency but not grammar ([Catani et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awt163)).
- **Stimulation during awake surgery.** Stimulating the left FAT made all eight patients in one series stutter; the tract was preserved and none stuttered afterwards ([Kemerdere et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-015-7949-3)). In five patients with left frontal glioma, stimulating white matter next to the tractography-defined FAT stopped speech in four and markedly slowed it in the fifth ([Fujii et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.3171/2014.10.JNS14945)). In 19 patients, stimulating the FAT or the nearby fronto-striatal tract interrupted movement or speech in all but one, and resection close to the left FAT went with transient trouble starting speech ([Kinoshita et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00429-014-0863-0)).
- **Damage.** In 23 glioma patients, FAT disruption tended to go with more severe early language deficits, including aphasic symptoms (one-sided p = 0.059, 17 patients), but not with lasting deficits, which tracked the arcuate fasciculus instead ([Nasiri et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bas.2026.106254)).
- **The cortex itself.** Aphasia and loss of movement after surgery near the midline are classically tied to the SMA, which lies mostly in AAL's Supp\_Motor\_Area rather than Frontal\_Sup ([Zentner et al., 1996](https://doi.org/10.3171/jns.1996.85.4.0542)); see Clinical significance.

## Other roles

Several functions often credited to “the SFG” come from next-door territory or are not specific to it.

- **Laughter (SMA).** Electrical stimulation of the anterior SMA in one patient with epilepsy evoked laughter, with smiling at lower currents ([Fried et al., 1998](https://doi.org/10.1038/35536)). That site lies mostly in AAL's Supp\_Motor\_Area rather than Frontal\_Sup.
- **Fluid intelligence.** In 227 patients with focal lesions and 165 controls, impaired fluid intelligence was confined to frontal lesions and was worse on the right. The right SFG stood out in a standard network analysis, but once lesion overlap was modelled separately, the effect sat in the right middle and inferior frontal gyri and the pre- and postcentral gyri ([Cipolotti et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awac304)).
- **Emotion regulation.** A lesion-derived network for emotion regulation (167 patients, checked in a further 629) centred on the left ventrolateral prefrontal cortex, not the SFG ([Jiang et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2023.02.007)).
- **Not established.** SFG activations appear in imaging studies of timing, reward, pain and creativity, but we found no study pinning these to the dorsolateral SFG. They are best listed as “reported; region assignment uncertain”.

## Receptor density profile

In post-mortem tissue, the atlas's values for this region exceed its precentral values for 14 of 15 receptors; α1 adrenoceptors are the one exception ([Visual Neuroscience receptor charts](https://visualneuroscience.ai/regions.html#receptors)).

- **Two data sets, two kinds of number.** In vivo maps (PET, plus one SPECT map for DAT) are group averages from one cohort per target (8–204 people), taken from the open compilation of Hansen et al.; Visual Neuroscience expresses each as a share of that target's densest region among 45 ([Hansen et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-022-01186-3)). Post-mortem autoradiography gives absolute densities for 15 receptors from three donor brains, cortex only ([Zilles & Palomero-Gallagher, 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnana.2017.00078)).
- **Compare within a data set.** PET values differ by tracer, so compare one target across regions, not targets against each other.

&#91;embedded content: Visual Neuroscience receptor layer · ex vivo autoradiography (Zilles & Palomero-Gallagher 2017), three post-mortem brains · 15 receptors; SFG = mean of areas 8 and 9, precentral = mean of areas 4 and 6\]

The atlas's SFG values are the mean of prefrontal areas 8 and 9 as sampled post-mortem, and its precentral values the mean of primary motor area 4 and premotor area 6 ([Visual Neuroscience](https://visualneuroscience.ai/regions.html#sel=P%3AFrontal_Sup)). The α1 exception comes from area 6, which has by far the highest α1 density of the 44 areas sampled, and motor areas 4 and 6 have among the smallest receptor fingerprints in the data set ([Zilles & Palomero-Gallagher, 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnana.2017.00078)). In that source's own analysis, AMPA, kainate, M3, 5-HT2 and D1 did not differ significantly between areas, and every value rests on three brains.

&#91;embedded content: Visual Neuroscience receptor layer · in vivo PET and SPECT maps compiled by Hansen et al. 2022 · 18 targets, share of each target's densest of 45 regions; tick = 20th-densest region\]

In vivo, the SFG falls outside the 20 densest of 45 regions for all 18 targets, though for nine of them by 5 points or less ([Visual Neuroscience](https://visualneuroscience.ai/regions.html#receptors)). The noradrenaline transporter reads 0% because the SFG value is below zero (BPND −0.086, against a thalamic peak of 0.24), meaning no measurable specific binding. The atlas lists 5-HT4 for only 14 regions, and this is not one of them.

- **Reading the shares.** For targets measured as total distribution volume or uptake ratios (CB1, NMDA, α4β2, H3, DAT, VAChT), part of the cortical signal is not specific binding, so a share is not a pure density ratio. The DAT map is a SPECT scan (\[123I\]FP-CIT) whose cortical signal is largely non-specific, and its pallidal peak likely reflects spill-over from the putamen; read that row with caution.
- **Monkey context.** In macaques, subdivisions of areas 8A and 8B have the lowest receptor densities in the prefrontal cortex, and orbitofrontal and frontopolar areas the highest ([Rapan et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.82850)). How far that carries over to human area 8 is unknown.

## Connections

Fibre tracts tie the SFG to the inferior frontal gyrus, the parietal and occipital lobes and the opposite SFG ([Briggs et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/ca.23523)). The lines below are tracts traced in human brains; tractography cannot tell which way signals flow.

&#91;embedded content: Main connections of the superior frontal gyrus · human tractography and dissection; sources below\]

- **Frontal aslant tract.** Fibres leave the outer SFG just in front of the precentral gyrus for the inferior frontal gyrus; none arose from the medial surface in 10 Human Connectome Project subjects, checked against dissections ([Briggs et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/ca.23523)). In 105 subjects, about 98% of its streamlines started on the crown of the gyrus, though the authors still conclude it arises from both surfaces ([Tagliaferri et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00429-024-02778-4)).
- **Inferior fronto-occipital fasciculus.** Fibres from the whole outer SFG run to the cuneus, lingual gyrus and superior parietal lobule ([Briggs et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/ca.23523)).
- **Callosal fibres.** Fibres from the front of the gyrus cross to the opposite SFG in the genu of the corpus callosum, and those from its back, including the SMA, in the anterior body; the study did not separate outer and medial faces ([Briggs et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/ca.23523)).
- **SLF I.** This dorsal branch links the superior parietal lobule to the posterior SFG and is tied to saccades, voluntary attention, imagery and motor sequences; its status as a separate tract is disputed ([Parlatini et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.08.031)).
- **Frontal partners.** The dorsolateral subregion connects with the middle and inferior frontal gyri ([Li W et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.04.011)).
- **Medial face and SMA.** The cingulum leaves from the medial surface for the precuneus and parahippocampal gyrus ([Briggs et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/ca.23523)). The posterior subregion that includes the SMA connects with the precentral gyrus, caudate, thalamus and frontal operculum ([Li W et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.04.011)). Both start outside AAL's Frontal\_Sup.
- **Monkey tracer work.** In macaques, area 9 receives no input from the lateral parietal cortex, whereas area 8B, behind it, receives prestriate and medial parietal input ([Petrides & Pandya, 1999](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1460-9568.1999.00518.x)).

## Clinical significance

Most clinical findings tied to “the SFG” come from its back end and medial face. Surgery there often causes a temporary SMA syndrome (loss of movement and speech initiation), and the psychiatric findings are correlational.

- **SMA syndrome after surgery.**
  - After 102 resections of posterior SFG tumours (the back quarter of the gyrus, SMA included), 47% developed a transient SMA syndrome and 3% had permanent new deficits ([Bauman et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.3171/2024.5.JNS231850)).
  - In 56 patients with SMA-region gliomas, 60.7% developed it: all whose FAT was resected versus 50% whose FAT was kept, a difference that missed significance (p = 0.06) ([Young et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3171/2021.4.JNS21187)).
  - Across 124 patients with superior or middle frontal gyrus tumours, it occurred in 43%: 47% for SFG, 40% for middle frontal and 27% for tumours in both, with no significant difference. This and the series above both come from the Mayo Clinic, so their patients may overlap ([Hsu et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wneu.2026.124844)).
  - In 28 SMA resections, 25 patients (89%) had new deficits right after surgery; they resolved within 3–42 days (mean 11), apart from minimal fine motor or speech problems in complex tasks ([Zentner et al., 1996](https://doi.org/10.3171/jns.1996.85.4.0542)).
  - In 13 patients with tumours in the dorsal SFG, fine motor skills were still worse three months after resection; there was no control group ([Maurer et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10143-024-02330-2)).
  - In 23 patients with the tract traceable on both sides, left–right asymmetry of the FAT on preoperative tractography went with the verbal form of SMA syndrome, but not with the syndrome overall ([Aliaga-Arias et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/cancers16223739)).
- **Working memory after surgery.** Because left SFG lesions cause load-dependent working-memory deficits ([du Boisgueheneuc et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awl244)), surgery on the lateral, posterior SFG may affect working memory. This is an inference, not a measured surgical outcome.
- **Schizophrenia.**
  - A meta-analysis of 8 studies (495 patients, 584 unaffected relatives, 596 controls) found less grey matter in the right SFG of patients, at Talairach 28, 60, 4, extending into the middle frontal gyrus. The cluster held in 7 of 9 leave-one-out reanalyses; relatives showed no change in the lateral SFG, and their prefrontal reduction was medial ([Xiao et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2013.00150)).
  - In schizophrenia starting before age 18 (7 studies, 199 patients), grey-matter volume was lower in small clusters labelled right SFG (area 9) and left SFG (area 6); the authors call the analysis low-powered and its threshold loose ([Liu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2022.785547)).
  - Patients with tardive dyskinesia (25) had a smaller right SFG (near area 8) than patients without it (25), and smaller volume went with more severe involuntary movements (r = −0.399). The cluster was picked from that same group contrast, which inflates the correlation, and it is smaller than the study's own corrected size threshold ([Li CT et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0071034)).
  - An early meta-analysis of 15 voxel-based studies (390 patients) found the most consistent deficits in the left superior temporal gyrus and medial temporal lobe, not the SFG ([Honea et al., 2005](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.162.12.2233)).
- **Paediatric OCD.** In OCD starting before age 18 (9 studies, 195 patients), grey matter was lower in a cluster labelled right SFG (area 8), at the border with the middle frontal gyrus, and higher in one labelled left SFG (area 10) ([Liu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2022.785547)).
- **Depression and brain stimulation (medial SFG).** The dorsomedial prefrontal cortex, on the medial surface (AAL Frontal\_Sup\_Medial), is “perhaps the most promising alternative target for rTMS” in depression ([Downar & Daskalakis, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brs.2012.08.006)). In an open-label series of 25 patients, dorsomedial rTMS lowered mean HAMD-17 scores from 21.3 to 12.0 ([Salomons et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/npp.2013.222)). In a sham-controlled trial with 52 completers, accelerated left dorsolateral then dorsomedial stimulation gave week-4 response rates of 57.69% versus 23.08% and remission rates of 38.46% versus 15.38%; blinding was limited because no sham coil was available, and with two targets the benefit cannot be assigned to the dorsomedial one alone ([Zhao et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2025.102402)).
- **Not established.** We found no evidence specific to Frontal\_Sup for ADHD, bipolar disorder, autism, Alzheimer's disease, frontotemporal dementia, epilepsy semiology, deep brain stimulation or brain–computer interfaces. These are research gaps, not negative findings.

## Open questions

The central question is whether “the SFG” should be treated as one region at all; the evidence says it should not.

- **Is Frontal\_Sup a unit?** It takes in agranular premotor cortex (6d2, with 6d3 in the sulcus at its edge) and prefrontal areas 8, 9 and 10, and AAL gives the medial part of its back to Supp\_Motor\_Area. AAL-labelled findings are best re-checked against Brainnetome or Jülich maps ([Ruland et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08528-4); [Fan et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhw157)).
- **How specific is its working-memory role?** fMRI suggests a spatial specialisation ([Courtney et al., 1998](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.279.5355.1347)), while lesions cause deficits across verbal, spatial and face material ([du Boisgueheneuc et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awl244)). The lesion study had eight patients from one centre and awaits replication.
- **Where exactly is the human FEF?** Functional studies place it at the back end of the superior frontal sulcus ([Paus, 1996](<https://doi.org/10.1016/0028-3932(95)00134-4>); [Amiez et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4739-05.2006)). Cytoarchitecture pairs it with premotor area 6v1, with earlier peaks scattered across 6d1, 6v1 and 6d3 ([Ruland et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08528-4)), and monkey–human homology is unsettled ([Vernet et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnint.2014.00066)).
- **Proactive or reactive control?** The right SFG's link to proactive restraint is correlational ([Hu et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1175-16.2016)), while reactive stopping maps to the right inferior frontal gyrus ([Aron et al., 2003](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1003); [Choo et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.79667)).
- **Does FAT damage cause lasting language problems?** Series point to transient trouble starting speech after surgery near the left FAT ([Kinoshita et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00429-014-0863-0); [Young et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3171/2021.4.JNS21187)). A 2026 series linked FAT disruption to early deficits including aphasic symptoms, on weak statistics, but not to lasting ones ([Nasiri et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bas.2026.106254)). Whether SLF I is a separate tract is also disputed ([Parlatini et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.08.031)).
- **Do the psychiatric findings belong to this region?** The grey-matter peaks are correlational, often anterior, reported in different coordinate spaces and at lenient thresholds ([Xiao et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2013.00150); [Liu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2022.785547); [Li CT et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0071034)).
