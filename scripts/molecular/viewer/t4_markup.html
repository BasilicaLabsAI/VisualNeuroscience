Every receptor here is a real molecule, drawn from the coordinates that crystallography and cryo-electron microscopy have measured: the five human dopamine receptors, D1 to D5, and the twelve serotonin receptors that signal through G proteins, from 5-HT1A to 5-HT7. Each sits in a patch of membrane with the outside of the cell above and the cytoplasm below. Pick a family and a receptor in the box, then follow the tour under Binding: the neurotransmitter arrives, finds the pocket between the helices, the receptor changes shape as it switches on, and the G protein docks beneath it. Drag to turn the molecule, scroll or pinch to zoom, and hover or tap a residue for what it is doing.

Drugs puts other molecules in the same pocket, each from its own structure: the antipsychotics that block D2, the agonists given in Parkinson’s disease, the triptans and ergots of migraine, and LSD and psilocin at 5-HT2A, with a note on whether each blocks the receptor or mimics its transmitter. Compare lays every subtype of a family side by side in the same state, with the residues of the binding pocket in a table. Where no structure of a receptor bound to its own transmitter exists, a stand-in agonist is used and named; the entry path of the ligand and the unresolved loops are drawn as illustration, and the change of shape between resting and active is an interpolation between the two measured states.

The Microanatomy page draws these receptors as schematics beside the transmitters that act on them, and the 3D Region Atlas maps where in the brain each is dense.

The helices, loops, side chains, neurotransmitters and drugs come from __NSTRUCT__ published structures of the human receptors. Each resting receptor is an antagonist- or inverse-agonist-bound structure with the ligand removed, since no structure of an empty receptor exists; where no resting structure has been solved, the receptor is shown only in its active shape. Where no structure with the neurotransmitter itself exists, an agonist stands in for it, as noted in the Binding tab.

Dopamine receptors

D1

Dopamine and fenoldopam, with Gs: 7CKZ (3.1 Å) and 7CKW (3.2 Å), cryo-EM. Xiao P, Yan W, Gou L, et al. Cell 184, 943–956 (2021). 7CKZ also contains the allosteric modulator LY3154207, which is left out.

Apomorphine with Gs: 7JVQ, cryo-EM at 3.0 Å. Zhuang Y, Xu P, Mao C, et al. Cell 184, 931–942 (2021).

D2

Dopamine with Go: 8U02, cryo-EM at 3.28 Å. Knight KM, Krumm BE, Kapolka NJ, et al. Nat Commun 15, 6643 (2024). Its Gαo carries K46E, a GNAO1 disease mutation that holds the complex in its nucleotide-free state.

Risperidone: 6CM4, X-ray at 2.87 Å. Wang S, Che T, Levit A, Shoichet BK, Wacker D, Roth BL. Nature 555, 269–273 (2018). Also the resting D2.

Haloperidol: 6LUQ, X-ray at 3.1 Å. Fan L, Tan L, Chen Z, et al. Nat Commun 11, 1074 (2020).

Bromocriptine with Gi: 7JVR, cryo-EM at 2.8 Å. Zhuang Y, et al. Cell 184, 931–942 (2021).

D3

Eticlopride: 3PBL, X-ray at 2.89 Å. Chien EYT, Liu W, Zhao Q, et al. Science 330, 1091–1095 (2010). Also the resting D3.

Pramipexole with Gi: 7CMU, cryo-EM at 3.0 Å. Xu P, Huang S, Mao C, et al. Mol Cell 81, 1147–1159 (2021).

D4

Nemonapride: 5WIU, X-ray at 1.96 Å. Wang S, Wacker D, Levit A, et al. Science 358, 381–386 (2017). Also the resting D4.

All five

Rotigotine with D1 to D5: 8IRR (D1), 8IRS (D2), 8IRT (D3), 8IRU (D4) and 8IRV (D5), cryo-EM at 2.7–3.2 Å, with Gs (D1, D5) or Gi (D2–D4). Xu P, Huang S, Krumm BE, et al. Cell Res 33, 604–616 (2023). These are the Compare tab, the rotigotine entries in the Drugs tab, and the active D3, D4 and D5.

How it was assembled

Coordinates come from GPCRdb. Each receptor’s structures are superposed on one another using their transmembrane helices (0.5–1.0 Å RMSD), and every receptor is placed in the membrane by matching its helix positions to D2’s (0.5–1.0 Å RMSD), where D2 itself sits with its most hydrophobic 30 Å band in the lipid tails. In the Compare tab only resolved residues are drawn.

Residues missing from every structure of a receptor (the N-terminus, most of loop 3, the tails) are drawn as beads on illustrative paths, not predictions. Short loops missing from one structure but present in another are borrowed and drawn pale. Sugars, the palmitoyl chain and the lipids are schematic. Fusion proteins, stabilising antibodies, nanobodies and modulators are left out; many constructs also carried thermostabilising point mutations, which are noted on hover.

The agonist’s path into the pocket and the morph between structures are straight interpolations for illustration, not simulations. Superscripts are Ballesteros–Weinstein numbers. D2 residues follow the long isoform, D2L, D4 residues the common 4-repeat form, and 5-HT4 and 5-HT7 the isoforms used in their structures (388 and 479 residues). 5-HT3, a ligand-gated ion channel rather than a GPCR, is not included.

Structures: Protein Data Bank entries, obtained through GPCRdb (gpcrdb.org), each cited above with its paper. Coordinates are public domain (CC0). Rendered with three.js (MIT).
